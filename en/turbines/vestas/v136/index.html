Model guide

Vestas V136

The large-rotor, low-to-medium-wind model of Vestas’ 4 MW platform: 136 m rotor, 66.7 m blades. The first common version was 3.45 MW, the later one 4.0–4.2 MW. In 2020 I worked on ground and nacelle assembly on a V136-3.45 MW project; the field notes on this page come from that job.

What defines this model

The energy a turbine captures grows linearly with swept area. The V136’s 14,527 m² is about 17% more than the V126 — on a weak-wind site that area is the biggest lever you have.

The price is paid in the blade: a 66.7 m blade is long, heavy and flexible. Root moment, blade-bearing load, transport and the crane plan all grow with it. The drivetrain follows the rest of the platform: a gearbox with two planetary and one helical stage and a full-scale converter.

Drivetrain and systems

Blade and blade bearing

  • On a long blade the pre-tension of root bolts and blade-bearing lubrication head the periodic work.
  • Even a few degrees of pitch difference between blades comes back as rotor imbalance and vibration; the physical angle is compared with the encoder value.

Assembly

  • Nacelle and blade lift windows are very sensitive to wind limits; it is usually the hours spent waiting on wind, not the mechanical work, that sets the assembly schedule.
  • Torque and tensioning sequences on flange and root connections follow the documentation and are marked; later checks rely on those marks.

Full-scale converter and cooling

  • All the power passes through the converter; its liquid cooling circuit and power connections are critical maintenance items.

Maintenance topics that matter in the field

  • On a vibration complaint, verify the pitch angles physically first; look at calibration before blaming the blade.
  • Blade-to-tower clearance and blade surface: periodic visual checks matter more on a long blade.
  • Converter and gearbox coolers: on a dusty site, radiator cleaning is one of the highest-return jobs.

About this page

Technical data come from documents published by the manufacturer; hub heights, wind class and cut-out differ between the 3.45 MW and 4.2 MW versions. For intervals and torque values the manufacturer’s service documentation governs.
