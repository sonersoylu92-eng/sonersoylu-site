Model guide

Vestas V126-3.45 MW

Vestas’ 3 MW-class (today’s 4 MW platform) medium-wind model. The first version was 3.3 MW, the common one is 3.45 MW; the rotor is 126 m. Unlike the V110 it uses a full-scale converter, so all the power passes through the converter.

What defines this model

The V126 was designed for medium-wind, high-turbulence sites (IEC IIIA / IIB). Its 12,469 m² swept area catches roughly a third more wind than the V110.

The drivetrain uses a gearbox with two planetary stages and one helical stage. The full-scale converter decouples the generator from the grid: grid events reach the generator less, while converter cooling and power-electronics maintenance move to the centre of the work.

Drivetrain and systems

Gearbox

  • Two planetary stages carry high torque in a compact housing; planet gear and bearing condition are followed through oil analysis and vibration trends.
  • Filter differential pressure, oil temperature and cooler cleanliness are read together.

Full-scale converter

  • Because all the power passes through the converter, its cooling circuit (fluid level, pump, fan, radiator) and power-connection torque are critical maintenance items.
  • A thermal scan under load catches loose connections that never show on a stopped turbine.

Hydraulic pitch

  • One pitch cylinder per blade. The accumulators and hydraulic unit hold the energy to feather the blades on grid loss.

Maintenance topics that matter in the field

  • Converter cooling: a dirty radiator or low fluid level is a common cause of temperature and overcurrent faults at high power.
  • Gearbox oil temperature and filter alarms: trend tracking and oil samples.
  • Hydraulic leaks and accumulator pre-charge.
  • Blade bearings and root bolts: periodic checks and lubrication.

About this page

Technical data come from documents published by the manufacturer and can change with variant, software package and site configuration. Generator type and some internal layout details differ between versions and are therefore not given here. For intervals and torque values the manufacturer’s service documentation governs.
