Model guide

Vestas V110-2.0 MW

The large-rotor member of Vestas’ 2 MW platform for low-wind sites: 2.0 MW rated power, 110 m rotor, 54 m blades. A classic geared architecture with a doubly-fed (slip-ring) generator; in the field most of the time goes into the gearbox oil system, the hydraulic pitch and the generator slip-ring side.

What defines this model

The V110 grows the rotor instead of the rating. A 2.0 MW ceiling on a 110 m rotor gives a turbine that reaches useful power early in light wind and targets a high capacity factor. Wind class IEC IIIA.

The architecture is familiar: rotor → main shaft → gearbox → high-speed shaft → doubly-fed generator. The stator connects directly to the grid, the rotor through slip rings to a partial-power converter. Each blade is pitched by its own hydraulic cylinder.

Drivetrain and systems

Gearbox and oil system

  • One planetary and two helical stages step the main-shaft speed up to generator speed.
  • What gets checked most: oil level and temperature trend, filter differential pressure, oil samples (metals and water), seals, and vibration trends for the bearings.
  • A temperature that drifts up at the same wind speed matters more than any single absolute value.

Doubly-fed generator (DFIG)

  • The rotor winding reaches the converter through slip rings and brushes, so brush wear, brush dust and slip-ring surface are periodic checks.
  • Because the stator is tied directly to the grid, grid events (voltage dips, imbalance) can come back as generator or converter faults.

Hydraulic pitch

  • A pitch cylinder turns each blade; pressure, accumulator pre-charge and hose and fitting tightness decide the health of the system.
  • The accumulators hold the energy to feather the blades on grid loss; testing them is a safety item that should never be skipped.

Maintenance topics that matter in the field

  • Hydraulic leaks in the hub and nacelle: a small drip becomes a dirty hub and an out-of-spec accumulator over time.
  • A slow rise in gearbox oil temperature: filter, cooler radiator and oil level are checked together.
  • Brush dust in the generator slip-ring compartment: cleaning and brush-length measurement are routine.
  • Yaw brake pads and gear lubrication: the first place to look for hunting and noise complaints.

About this page

Technical data come from documents published by the manufacturer and can change with variant, software package and site configuration. The maintenance topics summarise general field practice; for intervals and torque values the manufacturer’s service documentation always governs.
