From the field

Field Notes

These are not from a book; they came out of the tower. Each one follows a single fault from start to finish: what the alarm said, what I thought it was, which measurement I took, which possibilities it ruled out, what the root cause turned out to be and how we closed it. The point is not the answer — it is the order of the diagnosis.

A fault tree gives you a fast route from symptom to cause; these pages show you what it is like inside one case. The two complement each other: narrow it down with the tree first, then read the closest case here.

Note: these are field experience, not a substitute for the manufacturer's service documentation. Your own turbine's OEM instructions, LOTO procedure and site safety rules govern every intervention.

Case 01Nordex N117

Pitch motor high current

12 A → 18 AMotor current · alarm threshold 15 A

System
Pitch
Fault type
Mechanical
How it was caught
Alarm
Component
Pitch gearbox

Symptom“Pitch motor phase current high” alarm. The turbine cannot start in the morning; the pitch system never becomes ready.

Root causeA 2 mm crack in the gearbox housing. After months of oil loss the gears had been running dry.

Show the diagnostic path
  1. Symptom

    “Pitch motor phase current high” alarm. The turbine cannot start in the morning; the pitch system never becomes ready.

  2. Measurement

    Blades moved by hand: one blade stiffer than the others. Motor phase resistances 8.1–8.3 Ω — the motor is healthy.

  3. Root cause

    A 2 mm crack in the gearbox housing. After months of oil loss the gears had been running dry.

  4. Solution

    Gearbox drained, crack welded and checked by NDT, gears inspected; filter and oil renewed.

  5. Lesson

    The oil level had not been checked at the last service. The current rise was visible in the SCADA trend 8 months earlier.

Root causeThe shaft seal has failed; oil is leaking to the outer surface.

Show the diagnostic path
  1. Symptom

    Abnormal temperature trend for three days in 11–15 m/s wind. SCADA warns but the turbine keeps running.

  2. Measurement

    Oil level below the dipstick minimum. Oily dust around the breather filter.

  3. Root cause

    The shaft seal has failed; oil is leaking to the outer surface.

  5. Lesson

    The monthly checklist should include oil level, leak traces and the last 24 hours' peak temperature.

Case 03Enercon E92

Yaw hunting

±18°Yaw angle · 0.5–1 Hz oscillation

System
Yaw
Fault type
Control · Sensor · Mechanical
How it was caught
SCADA warning
Component
Yaw bearing · wind vane · PLC

SymptomIn 14 m/s wind the nacelle swings back and forth; the yaw angle in SCADA keeps changing, with a rhythmic knocking sound.

Root causeThree factors together: reduced bearing lubrication, a loose wind direction sensor and a PLC P gain above normal (1.5).

Show the diagnostic path
  1. Symptom

    In 14 m/s wind the nacelle swings back and forth; the yaw angle in SCADA keeps changing, with a rhythmic knocking sound.

  2. Measurement

    One-hour yaw angle trend: ±18°, 0.5–1 Hz. Yaw motor and angle feedback tested in test mode.

  3. Root cause

    Three factors together: reduced bearing lubrication, a loose wind direction sensor and a PLC P gain above normal (1.5).

  4. Solution

    P gain set to 0.6, sensor tightened and calibrated, bearing greased. Two weeks later the oscillation was ±2–3°.

  5. Lesson

    Hunting is rarely a single cause: software, sensor and lubrication are checked together.

Case 05Vestas V110

Converter overcurrent

17 / 19 eventsShare of faults above 2.4 MW

System
Converter
Fault type
Electrical
How it was caught
Alarm
Component
DC busbar · IGBT

SymptomIntermittent converter overcurrent fault. Normal after a reset; every measurement on the stopped turbine is normal.

Root causeA loosened DC busbar joint. It heats up at high current and also stresses the neighbouring IGBT module.

Show the diagnostic path
  1. Symptom

    Intermittent converter overcurrent fault. Normal after a reset; every measurement on the stopped turbine is normal.

  2. Measurement

    Fault log matched with power data. A thermal scan under load showed a DC busbar joint ~30 °C hotter than its neighbours; bolt torque below the OEM value.

  3. Root cause

    A loosened DC busbar joint. It heats up at high current and also stresses the neighbouring IGBT module.

  4. Solution

    Joint cleaned and tightened to OEM torque, every joint in the cabinet checked, the stressed IGBT replaced. Production continued meanwhile with a 2.2 MW limit.

  5. Lesson

    With an intermittent fault, look for the condition it appears in, not the fault itself. Do thermal scans while the turbine is producing.

Case 06Enercon E92

Yaw bearing wear

No alarmCaught during routine greasing

System
Yaw
Fault type
Mechanical
How it was caught
Field observation
Component
Yaw bearing lubrication line

SymptomThe turbine is producing normally. During greasing, dark grey metallic grease comes out of some points; a rubbing sound at one position.

Root causeThe automatic lubrication line is blocked; one sector of the bearing went without grease and wore.

Show the diagnostic path
  1. Symptom

    The turbine is producing normally. During greasing, dark grey metallic grease comes out of some points; a rubbing sound at one position.

  2. Measurement

    Grease from each point collected separately. Metal concentrated at three neighbouring points (a sector map); a magnet test confirmed steel particles.

  3. Root cause

    The automatic lubrication line is blocked; one sector of the bearing went without grease and wore.

  4. Solution

    Line cleared, the sector greased until clean grease came out, three months of short-interval follow-up. No bearing replacement needed.

  5. Lesson

    Collect purged grease point by point. A running pump does not mean grease reaches every point.

Blade vibration

1P · 2.5°In the 7–9 m/s band

Fault type

How it was caught
Field observation
Component
Pitch encoder

SymptomNacelle vibration felt only in the 7–9 m/s band. No visible damage on the blades.

Root causeCalibration drift in one blade's pitch encoder; the blade was running about 2.5° off.

Show the diagnostic path
  1. Symptom

    Nacelle vibration felt only in the 7–9 m/s band. No visible damage on the blades.

  2. Measurement

    Vibration recorded against rotor speed: dominant component 1P, 3P normal. Rotor imbalance.

  3. Root cause

    Calibration drift in one blade's pitch encoder; the blade was running about 2.5° off.

  4. Solution

    The encoder zero points of all three blades were reset against a physical reference. 1P dropped and production in that band returned to normal.

  5. Lesson

    Look at the spectrum before deciding on a blade inspection; measure the pitch angle physically.

Frequently asked

What should you do first when a turbine faults?

Before you read the alarm, make it safe: stop the turbine, apply LOTO, isolate the system you are going to touch. Then pull the OEM fault log and the SCADA trend. The trend tells you far more than a single instantaneous reading: did this start today, or has it been growing for weeks?

Why does a SCADA value differ from what you measure on site?

The most common reason is sensor calibration drift. RTD temperature sensors in particular change resistance over the years and SCADA then reports a value that does not exist. That is why the first move on a temperature alarm is to take a physical reading with an IR thermometer and compare: a large gap tells you the fault is in the sensor.
